=== Accessibility Statement ===

Unhurried is made to be read and used by everyone: with a keyboard, with the browser's zoom, with your own text spacing, with reduced motion, and in right-to-left languages. We work to WCAG 2.2 at level AA and to the WordPress accessibility-ready requirements, and we fix what we find in the next release. The theme has not yet been reviewed for the accessibility-ready tag, and it has not yet been tested with a screen reader.

== Quick Information ==

Accessibility audit completed: No

== Testing Tools and Methodology ==

Unhurried 1.2 was tested in November 2026 with automated tools and with scripts that drive real browser engines, run for the theme's author by software agents, on test sites holding the theme's demo pages and posts. No person tested it with assistive technology.

Automated tools:

* axe-core (WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2, levels A and AA) through Playwright in Chromium, on the theme's pages in all 20 looks, at phone and desktop widths.
* A contrast check of the theme's text and controls in all 20 looks, including their hover, focus and error states.
* Block validation of every template, template part and pattern in the Site Editor.

Scripted checks:

* Keyboard only, in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (Safari's engine): the skip link comes first, every stop shows a focus ring, and nothing hidden takes focus.
* Menus: the phone menu keeps focus inside while it is open and Escape closes it (Chromium); dropdowns open on hover and from the keyboard (Enter on their toggle), stay open while the pointer rests on them, and close with Escape (Chromium, Firefox and WebKit).
* Headings, landmarks and the names of controls, read from the browser's accessibility tree.
* Reflow: widths from 320 to 1920 pixels on a fresh load; and a page already open in a 1280-pixel window, narrowed to 640 and 320 pixels (the widths that 200% and 400% zoom give), in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, and in Chromium also with the WCAG text spacing settings. The window was narrowed; the browser's zoom itself was not used.
* Reduced motion (nothing moves), the browser's forced colors (high contrast) mode, printing, pages without JavaScript, and right-to-left languages.
* Mobile Safari on iPhone and iPad simulators: touch targets and page zoom up to 200%.

Not done yet:

* No test with a screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA, TalkBack). Hidden text and the names of controls were checked in the accessibility tree only.
* No testing with people who use assistive technology, and no outside audit.
* The browser's own zoom on a desktop (Ctrl or Cmd and +), and Firefox's Zoom text only setting.

While Unhurried 1.2 was built, the work was tested again, in rounds, by separate automated review passes before it was merged. These passes were run by software agents, not by people.

== Screen Reader Text Class ==

`.screen-reader-text`

Add it to a block of text, such as a paragraph or a heading, under Advanced, Additional CSS class(es): the block's text is hidden from sight and still read by screen readers. Keep links and buttons out of a block hidden this way: a link or button inside it still takes keyboard focus, and stays out of sight while it has it. A link that carries the class itself (written in a Custom HTML block, for one) becomes visible while it has keyboard focus, as the skip link does. The theme's own templates also use `.unhurried-visually-hidden`, which hides text the same way.

== Accessibility Features ==

* Motion follows the visitor's setting: with reduced motion, nothing moves. The moving line above the header and the homepage film each have a pause button. Add the class `unhurried-still` to a block to keep it and everything in it still for everyone: nothing in it enters, drifts with the scroll or moves by itself. What answers the pointer or the keyboard (a photograph easing closer, the footer wordmark's roll) still moves, and a film in it plays until it is paused.
* The focus ring shows for the keyboard on every surface and in every look, and not after a mouse click.
* Every text color pair in all 20 looks passes WCAG AA contrast. The six dark looks tell the browser the page is dark, so its own scrollbars and form fields turn dark too.
* Links in text are underlined, and the underline grows heavier under the pointer.
* On touch screens, the menu buttons, the header button, topic chips and the film's pause button take taps across 44 pixels.
* Right-to-left layouts mirror, and text over photographs keeps its contrast in both directions.
